Tracing fields, tracing commodities.

Trazo is an open machine-learning framework for mapping agricultural fields from satellite imagery. It includes a training dataset, four model checkpoints, and an inference pipeline for delineating agricultural field boundaries across South America. The first release includes 10,935,129 field boundaries and extends Fields of the World.

Coverage

The original benchmark included 1,854 field boundaries in South America. Trazo adds 46,908.

The Fields of the World benchmark included 1,854 field boundaries in South America, about 0.11% of its global corpus, drawn from a single site in Bahia, Brazil. Trazo extends that coverage to 17 soy-producing ecoregions, including the Andean foothills, the Chiquitania, the Pampas, Amazonian moist forests, the Chaco, and the Cerrado.

Models

Trazo provides four model checkpoints, each trained on a different combination of FTW and South American data for different use cases. The right checkpoint depends on the region, the agricultural system, and whether pixel-level or object-level accuracy matters more.

Trazo1
Initial SA adaptation

Best for large, uniform row-crop systems and boundary-sensitive tasks where subtle hard boundaries matter.

77.1% SA soy pixel IoU

Active learning · recommended

All four checkpoints share the same U-Net architecture with an EfficientNet-B3 backbone, inherited from FTW. For fine-tuning to new regions, see the repository README.

Pipeline

A sequence of composable utilities for moving from raw imagery to field-boundary predictions. Use the whole pipeline end-to-end, or borrow individual steps. Full reference in the repository README.

01

Create data

AOI grids and 4-band Sentinel-2 chips at planting and harvest windows.
02

Prepare data

Pair temporal stacks, normalize, build instance and semantic masks.
03

Sample & fine-tune

Active learning, temporal diversity, geographic balancing.

04

Train

U-Net, UperNet, DeepLabV3+, FCSiam, with twelve loss functions.
05

Infer at scale

Select the best Sentinel-2 scenes per tile and run multi-checkpoint inference at scale.
